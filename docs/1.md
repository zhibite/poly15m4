1. RSI (relative strength index, 14 period)
what it is ?
a momentum oscillator that runs from 0 to 100
measures how fast price has moved over the last 14 candles. invented by j. welles wilder in 1978 in his book new concepts in technical trading systems.

what it shows ?
extremes of buying or selling pressure.
when rsi is above 70, price has moved up too fast and is statistically more likely to stall or reverse.
below 30, the opposite.

how to use it on 15min btc ?
the mistake everyone makes: entering at rsi 75 expecting a reversal - wrong. 
price can stay overbought for hours.

what actually works:
rsi hits 75+ → wait → rsi drops back below 70 → that's your DOWN setup
rsi hits 25- → wait → rsi pops back above 30 → that's your UP setup
rsi sitting between 45 and 55 → flat market → skip, do not trade
divergence is the other use. price prints a new high but rsi prints a lower high. that's hidden weakness fade the new high.

2. MACD (12 / 26 / 9)
what it is ?
moving average convergence divergence. 
shows the relationship between a fast ema (12) and a slow ema (26), with a signal line that is a 9-ema of the difference. 
invented by gerald appel in 1979. the histogram was added by thomas aspray in 1986.
what it shows ?
direction and strength of momentum. 
not where price is, where it's heading.
how to use it on 15min btc?
the only rule that matters: trade in the direction of the histogram and avoid crossovers in flat markets.
histogram green and growing → UP momentum accelerating
histogram green but shrinking → UP momentum dying → do not enter UP
histogram red and growing → DOWN momentum accelerating
histogram contracting toward zero on either side → reversal warning, stay out
best entry: macd line crosses signal in the first 3-7 minutes of a 15-min window, in the same direction as your core trend.
ignore macd in flat markets (atr < $30 on btc). 
it lags too much there.
3. VWAP (volume-weighted average price)
what it is ?
the average price btc traded at over the session, weighted by volume at each price. introduced as a benchmark for institutional execution quality in the 1988 journal of finance paper by berkowitz, logue and noser.
what it shows ?
what big money paid on average. this is the line that funds, market makers and etf dealers benchmark against. price tends to revert to it.
how to use it on 15min btc
three setups that pay:
trend continuation: btc above vwap, macd green, rsi above 50 → UP
fade: btc far above vwap + rsi above 70 → reversion → DOWN
bounce: btc fell to vwap from above and bounced → support held → UP
vwap works best during the new york open session (13:30-16:00 utc). volume is high so the average is meaningful.
in asian session vwap is noisier.

4. EMA 9 and EMA 21
what it is ?
two exponential moving averages. 9-period reacts fast, 21-period reacts slow. ema gives more weight to recent prices than a simple moving average, so it turns faster.
what it shows ?
short-term trend direction. and more importantly, the moments where trend is shifting.

how to use it on 15min btc ?
best signal: ema crossover happens in the first 3-5 minutes of a 15min window. that's an early entry with the rest of the window to run.

worst signal: cross happens with 2 minutes left - too late, you're paying the spike - skip.
5. pivot points (HL10 - high/low of last 10 candles)

what it is ?
the highest high and lowest low of the previous 10 candles. originally used by floor traders before computers, who would calculate next day's pivot from yesterday's high, low and close on paper.
what it shows?
market memory. the zones where buyers stepped in last time (support) and sellers stepped in last time (resistance).
how to use it on 15min btc
four setups:
breakout up: btc closes above HL10 high with rising volume → UP
breakout down: btc closes below HL10 low → DOWN
bounce from support: btc touches HL10 low and reverses up → UP
rejection from resistance: btc touches HL10 high and reverses down → DOWN
worst trade: btc is in the middle of the HL10 range. 
that's chop do not enter.
best confirmation: pivot breakout aligned with macd crossover in the same direction two indicators screaming at you.

6. funding rate (perpetual futures basis)
what it is?
the periodic payment between long and short traders on perpetual futures contracts. 
positive funding = longs pay shorts. 
negative funding = shorts pay longs. 
paid every 8 hours on most exchanges, every 1 hour on some.
what it shows?
crowd positioning. when funding is extremely positive, the crowd is leveraged long and paying through the nose to stay there. when extremely negative, the crowd is short and bleeding.
extreme positioning is fuel for reversals.
how to use it on 15min btc
funding > +0.05% on binance perp btc → longs are over-extended → bias DOWN setups
funding > +0.10% sustained for 3+ funding periods → liquidation cascade risk → strong DOWN edge
funding < -0.05% → shorts over-extended → bias UP setups
funding near 0 → neutral, no edge
academic research backs this mean reversion strategies that fade extreme funding rates (above 95th percentile, historically above +0.12% for btc) have produced positive risk adjusted returns across exchanges.
think of it as a contrarian filter on top of your core 5.
if core 5 say UP but funding is +0.08%, cut your size in half.

7. CVD (cumulative volume delta)
what it is?
the running total of market buy volume minus market sell volume. market buys = orders that hit the ask. market sells = orders that hit the bid. cvd sums the net continuously.
it answers one question: who is actually aggressive, buyers or sellers?
what it shows?
real order flow underneath the price. price can stay flat while cvd quietly rises, which means buyers are absorbing every offer with no follow through yet. 
that's accumulation - cvd then leads the breakout.
how to use it on 15min btc
cvd rising + price flat → silent accumulation → expect UP breakout
cvd falling + price flat → silent distribution → expect DOWN breakout
price prints new high but cvd makes lower high → bearish divergence → fade the high → DOWN
price prints new low but cvd makes higher low → bullish divergence → fade the low → UP
cvd divergence is the strongest single advanced signal i use. 
the one indicator that consistently catches reversals before they show up on macd or rsi.

8. liquidation heatmap (coinglass)
what it is?
a visualisation of where leveraged long and short positions are clustered, and where they get liquidated if price reaches that level. coinglass calculates this from aggregate open interest, leverage usage and exchange data.
what it shows?
magnetic zones. price gets pulled toward dense liquidation clusters because when it gets close, forced liquidations trigger a cascade that accelerates price further in that direction.
bright yellow on the heatmap = densest cluster = strongest magnet.
how to use it on 15min btc
dense long liquidation cluster below current price → magnet pulling DOWN → bias DOWN setups
dense short liquidation cluster above → magnet pulling UP → bias UP setups
price approaching a cluster from above → expect acceleration DOWN as it touches
price already swept a cluster → cascade is done, expect counter-trend bounce
i use it as a target predictor more than an entry signal. 
if my core 5 say UP and there's a bright yellow cluster 0.3% above current price, that's the magnet i'm trading into.

9. order book imbalance (OBI)
what it is
the ratio of resting bid volume to resting ask volume at the top of the order book. measured at the top 5 or top 10 price levels on each side.
OBI = sum of bid sizes / sum of ask sizes.
what it shows
immediate buying or selling pressure. if there's a huge stack of bids and a thin stack of asks, the next aggressive seller eats the asks fast and price moves up. and vice versa.
how to use it on 15min btc
OBI > 1.5 at top 10 levels → bid-heavy book → short-term UP pressure
OBI < 0.67 → ask-heavy book → DOWN pressure
OBI = 1.0 ± 0.2 → balanced book → no edge from order book alone
academic research on multi-level order-flow imbalance shows that adding more price levels (not just the top of book) measurably improves how well imbalance predicts the next mid-price move. the result extends to crypto.
OBI is my fastest indicator. can change in seconds. i use it for the final go/no-go in the last 30 seconds before entry.

10. open interest (OI)
what it is
the total number of open perpetual futures and options contracts on btc that have not been closed. don't confuse with volume — volume counts contracts traded over a period, OI shows how many are open right now.
what it shows
how much money is actually in position. the difference between "people traded" and "people are holding". OI rising = new money entered. OI falling = old money left.
how to use it on 15min btc
classic rule (price + OI together):
price up + OI up → new longs entering with conviction → trend will continue UP
price up + OI down → shorts closing (short squeeze) → UP but no new fuel → expect reversal soon
price down + OI up → new shorts entering with conviction → trend will continue DOWN
price down + OI down → longs closing (capitulation) → DOWN but exhausting → expect bounce
best 15-min btc use:
volume spike + OI spike same direction = real signal. enter with trend
volume spike with no OI change = just rotation, noise. skip
OI works best paired with funding rate. funding shows where the crowd is leaning, OI shows how much ammo they have.
